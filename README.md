# Abstract

Inverted matter. An ancient charcoal-stone pyramid suspended upside down over a pale reflection, with four scroll-driven HTML projections.

[PLAN.md](PLAN.md) records the audiovisual review, research references, refinement decisions, and remaining release checks.

## Run

```sh
yarn install
yarn start
```

Open the local URL printed by Parcel (normally `http://localhost:1234`).

```sh
yarn build
```

The production site is written to `rel/`.

## The four compositions

1. **Pads / atmosphere** — wide chords establish the space that supports every later layer. The pyramid hangs on the right; its first face projects the content left. Six separate MIDI voices lift note-shaped patches of top paving into a slowly changing chord landscape. Notes keep stable spatial identities, with broad low-register patches and finer high-register patches.
2. **Short lead / motion** — the repeating short-note motif adds movement over the pads. The next face turns into view as the original camera path reframes the pyramid left and the content right. The SAME top-brick instrument and pitch map responds to sequence MIDI, with distinct short attack crests over quieter sustained pad relief. The original first two camera angles, rotation, and left/right switch are retained. This layer uses the supplied `sequence.mp3` stem and `sequence.mid`.
3. **Bass / weight** — bass and the separate kick enter together. Bass notes drive the third casing face; the low-passed, reverberant kick smoothly pulses the opened tip through its measured volume envelope, with no kick MIDI or onset trigger. Spectral green energy shines through the loosened joints and sends out uneven rays. Internal glow remains capped at 1.95 times its baseline. The downward DOM projector is separately restrained to 60% baseline opacity, 30% maximum pulse gain, and 5% maximum widening; the two triangular guides are also fainter. Physical opening and the passage/reveal gate remain scroll-driven.
4. **Long lead / melody** — the camera arcs above the square top as its middle descends into a suspended masonry gravity well. Rigid paving stones form deep steps around a small opaque black sphere with a thin, rapidly rotating green-white photon rim. Actual long-lead volume breathes its size and edge atmosphere. Joint pathfinding light is disabled on both top and sides in this chapter; the core takes visual focus. Musical relief remains quieter inside the well and stronger at the rim. A fixed HTML inscription wraps the view, with the title above and credits/link below an unobstructed central window. The square silhouette, original bird's-eye camera, white surroundings, cumulative music and supporting side displacement remain. This layer uses the supplied `lead.mp3` stem and `lead.mid`.

Each DOM composition gives a concise listening description and a static visual cue. Its real HTML link introduces the next layer; the final link returns to pads and fades the other layers away. Layout-dependent deep links are reapplied after styles and fonts settle.

## How it works

- `src/index.html` — dependency-free first-paint pyramid loader, matching inline-SVG header mark, semantic chapters, navigation, and motion control.
- `src/scripts/main.js` / `src/scripts/startup.js` — lightweight bootstrap, deferred scene import, real initialization milestones, first-frame/audio readiness, an explicit play-entry gate, and a single full-white reveal flash.
- `src/scripts/audio-mixer.js` — five sample-synchronized native Web Audio loops across four chapters, cumulative mixing, reversible gain fades, bounded master headroom, and an independent sound control.
- `src/scripts/visual-clock.js` / `src/scripts/feature-history.js` — guarded output-device visual timing and fixed-storage interpolation of measured stem/gain features; playback scheduling is unchanged.
- `src/scripts/frame-budget.js` — sustained-frame-pressure detection that reduces optional decoration, never musical timing, stone geometry, or the reading plane.
- `src/scripts/audio-features.js` — per-stem native analysers, calibrated RMS envelopes, low-frequency energy, and smoothed spectral brightness. Note attacks come from the existing MIDI evaluator; unused spectral-flux detection has been removed.
- `src/scripts/midi-score.js` — dependency-free Standard MIDI File reader retaining tracks, note identity, velocity, tempo maps, running status, and sustain; 172 BPM is the explicit default for these tempo-less exports.
- `src/scripts/midi-player.js` — stateless audio-clock score evaluation, six independent pads voice profiles, shared note-to-surface mapping, attack/hold/release envelopes, and loop-boundary release handling.
- `src/scripts/bass-resonance.js` / `src/scripts/kick-resonance.js` — existing collision-prepared spatial regions and their smooth, fixed-profile kick-envelope weighting for the opened mechanism.
- `src/scripts/stone-surface.js` / `src/scripts/stone-motion.js` — small shared displacement field, matching GPU/CPU bilinear sampling, and rigid top-stone translation in the color and shadow shaders. No extra light meshes or render passes.
- `src/scripts/crown-well.js` / `src/scripts/well-lining.js` / `src/scripts/well-masonry.js` / `src/scripts/well-core.js` / `src/scripts/well-orbits.js` / `src/scripts/well-haze.js` — reversible crown descent, buried-course clearance and gap-sealing backing, smaller stepped interior bricks, a lead-reactive black horizon with white orbital ribbons, and scene-depth-aware green haze.
- `src/scripts/choreography.js` — chapter emphasis separate from audibility, retaining earlier instruments as quieter support without applying audio gains twice.
- `src/scripts/seam-network.js` / `src/scripts/seam-flow.js` — real casing/crown joint connectivity, deterministic musical travelers, lip-registered emissive ribbons, and localized stone-edge spill in the main and mirrored views.
- `src/scripts/pyramid-reflection.js` — a simple six-triangle pyramid mirrored below Y=0, following the live sculpture pose; weighted copies wash out the edges in one draw, without a second scene render, reflection texture, ripples or pond motes.
- `src/styles/main.scss` — white editorial layout, projected typography, inverse final section, and static fallback.
- `src/scripts/scroll.js` / `src/scripts/viewport.js` — one native document-scroll timeline with a shared stable framing height. On coarse-pointer devices, the small viewport is measured once per width/orientation; toolbar-only height changes cannot move chapter snap targets or reframe the camera. The large viewport is measured at the same time to pre-reserve blank trailing space, keeping the final snap reachable even before Safari reports a toolbar resize. That space is retained when the toolbar reappears; visual-viewport resize events are also observed. Real width/orientation changes and desktop window resizes still update the frame. Viewport-fixed panels share Chrome's native document scroll chain; no wheel interception or pointer-event disabling is used.
- `src/scripts/sketch.js` — reversible pyramid rotation, lift, camera orbit, and composition. Live frames also sample actual document scroll position when it differs from the last event, avoiding dependence on WebKit's event delivery during native touch momentum/snapping. Structural transforms remain derived directly from scroll progress; local musical displacements follow isolated stem features, without independent timed tweens or pointer lag.
- `src/scripts/projections.js` — HTML panels attached to face-local Three.js rigs. A CSS pixel is mapped to a world-space plane, then through the same view/projection matrices used by WebGL. Homogeneous `matrix3d()` transforms preserve real perspective and camera registration.
- `src/scripts/masonry-layout.js` — deterministic, staggered stone courses, curated missing-block wounds, and a real internal chamber. Block IDs and layout do not depend on the viewport.
- `src/scripts/masonry.js` — twelve instanced geometry batches, weathered stone materials, lower-detail mobile geometry, surviving-face anchors, conservative passage clearance, and bounded moving-instance uploads.
- `src/scripts/tip-burst.js` — reversible scroll-driven tip separation, precomputed neighbour/mode clearance, slow suspended-fragment circulation, and smooth kick-envelope expansion. No running physics simulation.
- `src/scripts/tip-energy.js` — recessed, depth-masked energy lining and elongated core, actual-stone joint bindings, fragment-clipped shafts, and short-range green diffuse spill. No central glowing sphere or circular aperture sprite.
- `src/scripts/sculpture.js` — masonry monument, chapter lighting, energy/beam integration, face anchors, and the lightweight mirrored pyramid silhouette.
- `src/scripts/atmosphere.js` — continuous face-to-corner guide lines, four-corner projection frames, shallow particle backings, and sparse ambient dust. Frames and connections use the actual projected panel planes, including the mobile and top-face poses.
- `src/scripts/shaders/` — restrained downward emission, softened reflection, shallow particle fields, and film finish. A dedicated finite-value pass clamps HDR texels **before** bloom convolution, preventing invalid values from spreading into black rectangles.
- `src/scripts/app.js` — cached PCF shadows, HDR-only soft-knee bloom extraction, multi-scale chromatic halation, highlight roll-off, subtle grain, output color conversion, final-pass FXAA, capped pixel density, and visibility/context lifecycle. Floating-point targets are not multisampled.

Resolution tuning prioritizes the approved image quality: the user reported no meaningful performance gain from lower-resolution profiles and found the pyramid visibly degraded. The fixed desktop pixel-area budget is removed, and the earlier density ceilings are restored: **1.25 DPR compact / 1.5 DPR wide**, without viewport-size-based downscaling. Shadows remain **1024×1024 compact / 2048×2048 wide**. The old **512×512 compact / 1024×1024 wide** reflection targets are no longer needed by the simple mirrored silhouette. Reduced haze integration remains **six samples compact / eight wide** (six under sustained frame pressure), and all-composition GPU preload remains in place. Native scrolling, main sculpture geometry, bloom, motion, audio and frame scheduling are unchanged; there is no FPS cap. Further performance work should measure the bottleneck instead of lowering resolution again by default.

The floor reflection is now a deliberately simple second pyramid, not a mirrored scene or cached image. Twenty-five small Gaussian-weighted copies soften its silhouette in one draw (150 tiny triangles total); pale shading washes away toward the deeper end. It does not copy masonry, the opening, the well or musical brick motion. Motion-off uses the same stationary chapter pose as the sculpture. There is no reflection render target or additional camera/pass, and no scrolling-triggered fade. The original scroll-driven shadow refresh is retained; the rejected scroll shadow pacing remains removed. Main-view and shadow resolution are unchanged. Physical-device FPS gains remain unmeasured. Older mirrored-water validation notes below describe the superseded floor.

The full-screen paper-white loader is authored directly in HTML with critical inline CSS, the same solid black faceted inverted-pyramid SVG used in the header, and a green hairline stage indicator. It does not need the 3D pyramid, external fonts, images, or the main stylesheet to appear. The stylesheet loads without blocking that first paint; JavaScript waits for the loader to paint before importing the heavy scene bundle.

Loading progress reports actual initialization stages, not fabricated download percentages: scene assets, procedural stone/textures, font/layout readiness, five decoded sound channels, then shader preparation and a complete rendered frame including reflection and postprocessing. Once everything is ready, that same inverted-pyramid mark rotates smoothly by 90 degrees into a right-pointing play position over .8 seconds, without swapping or fading out the icon. A circular outline, hover/press feedback, **Play & enter** label, and **Click to start the music** instruction make the 96-pixel button explicit. The full-screen gate also offers a small **Enter silently** alternative; waiting for the visitor has no timeout. The scene stays hidden and inert until entry, with no audio autoplay and no artificial minimum loading duration. Clicking play starts the mixer through that native user gesture, then reveals the prepared scene through one pure-white flash: roughly 50 ms fully white, followed by a fast release, 280 ms total. The effect is DOM-only, with no new render pass or altered scene exposure. Reduced motion or motion-off skips the flash and loader animation.

GPU preparation visits **all four compositions behind the opaque loader**, not just the entry view. Three.js compiles hidden materials but gathers only visible lights, so the opened tip's three point lights and the well's point light need their own lighting variants. Compilation targets the composer's actual linear HDR buffer; real warmup draws upload current-layout geometry, instance buffers and textures, including silent musical effects, and initialize shadows, reflection and all postprocessing passes. An asynchronous GPU fence completes before entry becomes available. Temporary render flags are restored even on failure; the actual scroll/deep-link pose is restored, no music is started, and warmup does not advance animation or independent-drop clocks. Native scrolling and render quality are unchanged. This removes first-use work, not the ongoing rendering cost of the later compositions; a layout change or context restoration can still require GPU resource initialization.

Background pages pause the startup watchdog. Fonts have a five-second readiness budget; a failed scene startup or a twenty-second visible-page timeout releases the existing static-art fallback instead of trapping navigation behind a loader. Audio preparation has a fifteen-second budget and can fail independently: the ready gate still lets the visitor enter without sound. An interrupted audio-resume request times out after four seconds and keeps retry/silent-entry controls usable. JavaScript-disabled visitors get the static layout without the loader. During startup the underlying UI is inert and the loader announces stages; entry restores interactivity and retains native-scroll/deep-link positioning.

## Five-channel sound, four cumulative layers

The five supplied `assets/audio/{pads,sequence,bass,lead,kick}.mp3` files are used and copied into the production bundle. Bass is now isolated from kick; kick is a separate channel owned by the existing third chapter, with no MIDI file. Each export has two equal halves: an introduction and a repeating section. Playback is **first half → second half → second half → …**. Each native `AudioBufferSourceNode` starts at offset zero, with `loopStart` at the common decoded duration's midpoint and `loopEnd` at its full end. The first half plays once; the initial transition into the second half is ordinary continuous buffer playback. Only the end wraps back to the midpoint. There is no JavaScript restart, duplicate music source, inserted pause, or scroll-triggered replay.

All five files are decoded once while the audio context is suspended, and mismatched durations are rejected rather than allowed to drift. An interactive-latency context avoids requesting the larger playback-prefill preset. The audible master route stays disconnected while the device resumes and renders a short, literal-zero warm-up buffer. A separate permanently zero-gain sink keeps the graph advancing while that route is closed. Once warm-up ends, master and channel gains are re-anchored at zero on the running clock. The five music sources start together 120 ms ahead with identical intro and loop boundaries; channel fades and a separate 180 ms master fade are scheduled before the audible route connects.

The decoded intro also has an independent PCM safeguard: its first 20 ms are silent, followed by a smooth ramp to the original samples at 180 ms. This modifies only the in-memory intro head, not the MP3 files, duration, sample offsets, existing peak/headroom bounds, or any sample in the repeating half. Muted stems continue playing; scrolling never seeks, stops, recreates, or restarts a channel. Sound enabled after silent entry starts the shared introduction once; subsequent mute/unmute or chapter changes do not reset it.

Safari decoded each two-part export to **4,286,512 stereo frames at 48 kHz**, or **89.30233333333334 seconds**. The midpoint and repeating period are both **44.65116666666667 seconds** (2,143,256 frames). Before the PCM startup safeguard, a one-off native offline rendering check compared sampled PCM throughout the initial file and at two successive loop wraps for all four stems: each matched the expected original/full-file or second-half samples with zero error. A subsequent comparison confirmed the guarded buffers are unchanged after 180 ms and throughout the entire repeating half. Live Safari AudioWorklet capture measured zero output before source start and through the 20 ms opening silence; the first second matched the intended fade within approximately `1.1e-8` sample amplitude. These checks verify the browser signal, not acoustic silence or device-driver behavior. Forward/reverse chapter checks retained all four source objects, the shared start, and the existing gain targets.

## Seeing each stem

Each source passes through its own transparent native `AnalyserNode` before its channel fader. Analysis never sees the combined mix and does not change the PCM, gain ceilings, source offsets, or loop boundaries. Reused typed arrays hold 2048-frame waveforms and spectra. Reads are capped at 60 Hz on wide layouts and 30 Hz on compact layouts; no analysis-specific animation loop or full-screen render pass is added.

Fixed pre-fader RMS ranges, calibrated against the supplied exports, retain rests instead of auto-normalizing background noise into a pulse. Bass measures the 30–220 Hz share of its energy; smoothed low/mid/high spectral proportions subtly shape the surface patches. MIDI supplies note identity and precise onset/hold timing, while the audio envelope supplies actual strength and release-tail attenuation. This is not FFT pitch transcription or a physically literal vibration at each audible frequency. Actual interpolated channel and master gain automation scales every visual feature through startup, reverse scrolling, and mute fades.

Kick uses its isolated **20–180 Hz RMS envelope**, normalized over `.008–.30`, with **45 ms attack / 180 ms release** smoothing. The tip follows that continuous level directly: no spectral-flux trigger, beat timer, peak latch, MIDI, or fixed-strength flashes. Its filtered/reverberant volume crests produce smooth outward breaths and natural decays. Bass never contributes to these musical tip pulses, and kick never drives the bass casing paths.

All four existing `assets/midi/*.mid` exports are retained for both musical phases, as requested. They omit tempo metadata; the song's confirmed **172 BPM** converts 128 beats / 32 bars into 44.6511628 seconds. The score repeats every common half-duration during the introduction and repeating section; it is not stretched to the full two-part file or independently to a stem's last note. Pads contains six tracks and 48 notes: `tremoloed saw` (16), `pad synt cello` (2), `pad synt violin` (6), `smooth pad` (15), `shiny gated pad` (7), and `drone pad` (2). The score evaluator reads `(visualAudioTime - sharedStart) % audioLoopDuration`; no independent MIDI timers, event queues, or scrolling-driven note restarts exist. When valid, `AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp()` maps the estimated output-device position to the performance clock. Startup zeroes, stale/invalid reports, and offsets beyond .5 seconds fall back to `currentTime`; evaluations cannot rewind during a running interval. No guessed Bluetooth delay or double-counted `outputLatency` is added. Sources and gain automation still use the original context clock. A 64-sample typed-array history per channel, recorded at most 60 Hz, interpolates measured features and gain envelopes to that same visual time. A live interpolation endpoint preserves frame-by-frame fades without letting high-refresh displays exhaust the history. Suspension, context loss, hidden pages, and motion-off clear histories; missing history cannot borrow a future attack. This is output-aware timing, not a claim of measured acoustic synchronization on every device.

Past-cycle tails are evaluated only after a real loop, so startup cannot replay future notes. Missing/invalid MIDI suppresses that stem's note geometry without preventing audio playback; bass retains an envelope-only fallback. Explicit MIDI tempo events, when present, take precedence over the song default. The parser also derives four-bar phrase onset densities through the same tempo map. Density changes patch breadth by only 6%, never fabricating a note, pulse, or activity during a rest.
